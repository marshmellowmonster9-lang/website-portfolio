Unity 6 Environment Art Technical Art Level Design

Overview

Rogue Directive: Genesis is a sci-fi action game developed in Unity 6 as part of my final-year university project at the University of Gloucestershire, where it received the Best Group Project 2026 award. As an Environment Artist and Level Designer, I was responsible for the environment art and level design of Level 2: Orbital Breach and Level 3: The Vault. I developed both environments from initial blockout through to their final playable implementation, including modular assets, props, materials, lighting and environment dressing.

“My aim was to create environments that balance visual quality with clear gameplay spaces and real-time performance.”

Key Details

  • Role: Environment Artist, Level Designer & Technical Artist
  • Tools: Unity 6, Shader Graph, Blender, Substance Painter, Krita
  • Focus: Modular environments, props, trim sheets, shaders, lighting, level design and optimisation

Technical breakdown

Technical breakdowns covering modular workflows, materials, shaders, lighting and optimisation.

Modular Asset Showcase

A modular asset kit was developed around a consistent grid to support reuse across multiple spaces while maintaining consistent proportions and visual language. This workflow allowed environments to be constructed efficiently while providing flexibility for different layouts and gameplay requirements.

Bio Vegetation Vat

A bespoke environment prop designed to support both environmental storytelling and gameplay. The Bio Vegetation Vat features breakable glass and was designed to fit within the wider biotechnology facility aesthetic.

Lighting & composition

Lighting was baked in Unity and combined with post-processing to establish the final visual presentation. The breakdown compares the final scene against the unprocessed scene and Unity's lighting debug view, showing how the lighting setup contributed to the final result.

Trim Sheets

Structural assets were created using a shared trim sheet workflow to maintain consistent texel density and maximise material reuse. This reduced the number of unique textures required across the environment while maintaining visual consistency across modular assets.

Hologram Shader Graph

A custom Shader Graph material was created for the holographic displays, using animated scan lines and a Fresnel effect to create a dynamic sci-fi material.

Parallax Occlusion Mapping

A custom material using Parallax Occlusion Mapping was used to create the appearance of deeper surface detail without adding additional geometry. This allowed detailed wall surfaces to be created while keeping the environment lightweight and suitable for real-time rendering.
